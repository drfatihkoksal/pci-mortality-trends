# Literature search log

Search date: 2026-08-15  
Sources: PubMed/MEDLINE, PubMed Central, publisher records, Crossref/DOI records  
Type: targeted narrative search; not a systematic review

## Concepts

1. `percutaneous coronary intervention AND temporal trends AND mortality AND registry`
2. `PCI AND long-term all-cause mortality AND registry`
3. `Turkey OR Türkiye AND PCI AND mortality`
4. `routinely collected health data AND RECORD statement`
5. `STROBE cohort reporting`

## Retained evidence anchors

1. Dawson et al. *Temporal Trends in Patient Risk Profile and Clinical Outcomes
   Following Percutaneous Coronary Intervention.* Cardiovascular
   Revascularization Medicine. 2021;31:10–16. Registry cohort; directly informs
   case-mix interpretation. Access basis: abstract/publisher record. DOI verified:
   10.1016/j.carrev.2020.12.019.
2. Asano et al. *Temporal trends in clinical outcomes after percutaneous coronary
   intervention: a systematic review of 66,327 patients from 25 all-comers
   trials.* EuroIntervention. 2022;17:1318–1329. Systematic review; demonstrates
   divergent temporal patterns across outcomes. Access basis: full text. DOI
   verified: 10.4244/EIJ-D-21-00192.
3. Hulme et al. *Temporal trends in relative survival following percutaneous
   coronary intervention.* BMJ Open. 2019;9:e024627. National registry study;
   emphasizes demographic case-mix and background mortality. Access basis: full
   text. DOI verified: 10.1136/bmjopen-2018-024627.
4. Kilickap et al. *Short and Midterm Outcomes in Patients With Acute Myocardial
   Infarction: Results of the Nationwide TURKMI Registry.* Angiology.
   2021;72:339–347. Turkish national benchmark. Access basis: abstract/publisher
   record. DOI verified: 10.1177/0003319720975302.
5. Takeji et al. *Sex Differences in Clinical Outcomes After Percutaneous
   Coronary Intervention.* Circulation Journal. 2023;87:277–286. Large registry
   study showing that sex comparisons depend on presentation. Access basis:
   abstract. DOI verified: 10.1253/circj.CJ-22-0517.
6. Benchimol et al. *The REporting of studies Conducted using Observational
   Routinely-collected health Data (RECORD) Statement.* PLOS Medicine. 2015.
   Reporting guideline; full text. DOI verified: 10.1371/journal.pmed.1001885.

## Limitations and update rule

Coverage is targeted rather than exhaustive, and some retained clinical studies
were assessed from abstracts. Before submission, repeat the searches from 2024
onward, screen contemporary national/PCI registries, verify every retained DOI
against a canonical record, and document any paywalled full texts that could not
be assessed.
